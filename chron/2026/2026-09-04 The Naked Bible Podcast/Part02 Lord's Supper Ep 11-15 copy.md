# Part 2, The Lord's Supper, Episodes 11-15

**The Naked Bible Podcast: The Lord’s Supper Series, Episodes 11–15**

**Episode 11: “Introducing the Lord’s Supper Series”**  
Heiser introduces communion, also called the Lord’s Supper or Lord’s Table. He questions common teachings such as the literal or spiritual presence of Jesus in the elements, communion as a “means of grace,” restricting children, and the need to confess sin immediately before partaking. He identifies Matthew 26, Mark 14, Luke 22, and 1 Corinthians 11 as the key biblical passages, while warning that John 6 may not belong in the discussion.

**Episode 12: “The Lord’s Supper and the Gospels, Part 1”**  
He begins examining John 6, especially Jesus’ teaching about being the “bread of life.” He argues that John 6 is not the Last Supper because the chronology and details do not match the Synoptic Gospel accounts. Jesus’ language about eating and drinking is interpreted metaphorically: “eating” represents coming to Jesus and believing in him. Eternal life comes through faith in Christ, not through physically consuming bread and wine.

**Episode 13: “The Lord’s Supper and the Gospels, Part 2”**  
He completes his analysis of John 6:41–65. He argues that the references to eating Jesus’ flesh and drinking his blood must be read in light of the earlier statements equating eating with believing. Jesus’ flesh points to his sacrificial death on the cross, not to the communion elements. He also emphasizes that John 6 is separate from John’s Last Supper account in chapter 13 and therefore should not control the doctrine of communion.

**Episode 14: “The Lord’s Supper and 1 Corinthians 8–11, Part 1”**  
He turns to Paul’s teaching, beginning with the context of 1 Corinthians 8–10. Paul’s discussion of food sacrificed to idols, Old Testament sacrifices, and fellowship with God or demons provides the background for chapter 11. Heiser explains *koinonia* as participation or fellowship and argues that the Lord’s Supper is a communal meal involving fellowship with God. It is not a repeated sacrifice, and participants do not receive saving grace by eating the elements.

**Episode 15: “The Lord’s Supper and 1 Corinthians 8–11, Part 2”**  
He examines 1 Corinthians 11:17–34 and argues that the early Lord’s Supper included a substantial fellowship meal. Paul’s concern was that wealthy Corinthians were eating excessively, getting drunk, neglecting the poor, and creating divisions. “Eating and drinking in an unworthy manner” therefore refers primarily to abusing the communal meal and humiliating fellow believers, rather than merely failing to confess private sins. The stated purpose of the Supper is to remember and proclaim Jesus’ death until he returns.

**Overall Summary**  
Across Episodes 11–15, Heiser argues that the Lord’s Supper should be understood from the biblical text rather than later sacramental traditions. Its central purposes are remembrance, proclamation of the gospel, and fellowship among believers. He rejects the idea that communion itself provides salvation, forgiveness, or mystical grace, and emphasizes the importance of unity and care for fellow Christians.
