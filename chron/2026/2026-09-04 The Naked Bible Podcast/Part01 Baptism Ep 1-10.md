# Part 1, Baptism, Episodes 1-10

(Asked AI to summarize episodes on Baptism, September 4, 2026 - 11:29 PM 27s • GPT-5.6 Luna • 2.8 credits)

**The Naked Bible Podcast, Part 1: Episodes 1–10**

**Episode 1: “Baptism: What You Know May Not Be So”**  
Heiser introduces the baptism series and distinguishes believer’s baptism from infant baptism. He argues that many traditions create tension between baptism and salvation by faith alone. Using Colossians 2:11–12, he emphasizes that baptism must be interpreted consistently with circumcision, which did not erase sin, guarantee salvation, or guarantee future faith.

**Episode 2: “Contradictions in Creeds, Part 1”**  
He examines the Belgic Confession. Although it clearly teaches salvation through Christ and faith apart from human merit, its baptism section appears to associate baptism with belonging to Christ, spiritual birth, and continuing grace. He argues that this creates unresolved problems when baptized people later abandon the faith.

**Episode 3: “Contradictions in Creeds, Part 2”**  
He analyzes the Heidelberg Catechism, focusing on its language about baptism as a “sign and seal” of forgiveness, regeneration, and eternal life. He discusses Titus 3:5 and Acts 22:16, arguing that these passages do not teach that water baptism itself saves. He also highlights the Catechism’s difficulty explaining how baptized infants can later reject Christianity.

**Episode 4: “Contradictions in Creeds, Part 3”**  
He critiques the Westminster Confession’s teaching on baptism. The Confession calls baptism a sign and seal of regeneration, remission of sins, and union with Christ, while also denying that every baptized person is regenerated. He argues that its language about grace being “conferred” through baptism conflicts with salvation by faith and cannot be supported by what Scripture says about circumcision.

**Episode 5: “Baptism, Circumcision, and Biblical Theology”**  
He presents his proposed solution. Circumcision did not save people or produce faith; it marked membership in Israel, the community entrusted with knowledge of God and his promises. He argues that baptism functions similarly: it places someone within the believing community where they can hear the gospel and be formed in faith. Baptism is therefore connected to community membership, not automatic salvation.

**Episode 6: “Applying the Baptism-Circumcision Theology to Adult or Believer’s Baptism”**  
He applies the same framework to adult baptism. Believer’s baptism can publicly express faith already present, but the ritual itself does not save. He argues that the New Testament clearly records adult believers being baptized, but does not clearly prohibit infant baptism. Arguments for or against infant baptism based solely on New Testament baptism narratives therefore go beyond what the texts establish.

**Episode 7: “The Mode of Baptism and the Biblical Text”**  
He considers immersion, sprinkling, and pouring. Examining the Greek words *baptizo* and *bapto*, he concludes that they can refer to dipping or immersion but are not always limited to that meaning. He also questions whether Romans 6 is describing water baptism at all. Since the biblical evidence does not conclusively establish one required method, he argues that the mode of baptism is secondary.

**Episode 8: “Baptism and Problem Passages: 1 Peter 3:14–22”**  
He explains the difficult connection between baptism, Noah’s flood, imprisoned spirits, and salvation. Drawing on the worldview reflected in 1 Enoch, he argues that Peter presents Jesus as proclaiming victory over the fallen spiritual powers. Baptism is described as a pledge of loyalty to the risen Christ and a public declaration in the cosmic conflict between God and evil, rather than as a ritual that mechanically removes sin.

**Episode 9: “Baptism and Problem Passages: Acts 22:16”**  
He examines the command to Paul to “be baptized and wash away your sins, calling on his name.” His grammatical analysis focuses on the Greek middle voice and the relationship between the actions in the sentence. He concludes that the passage emphasizes Paul’s personal decision to call on Christ and be baptized, not water acting independently to remove sin. Faith and baptism occur together, but baptism is not the saving cause.

**Episode 10: “Baptism and Problem Passages: Acts 2:38”**  
He addresses Peter’s command to repent and be baptized “for the forgiveness of sins.” He argues that baptism must not be separated from the preceding command to repent. The Greek preposition *eis* can mean “for the purpose of” or “because of/on the basis of,” and Heiser favors the interpretation that repentance leads to forgiveness, while baptism follows as an outward expression of that changed allegiance. Baptism does not itself confer forgiveness.

**Overall Summary**  
Episodes 1–10 develop a consistent argument: baptism is an important biblical sign of identification with God’s people, public loyalty to Christ, and participation in the believing community, but it is not the mechanism of salvation. Heiser repeatedly uses the baptism-circumcision relationship to challenge claims that baptism automatically removes sin, guarantees election, produces faith, or confers salvation.
