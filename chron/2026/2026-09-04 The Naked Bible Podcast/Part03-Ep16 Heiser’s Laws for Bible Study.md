# Episode 16: Heiser’s Laws for Bible Study: Learning to Study the Bible, Part 1

1. There is no substitute for close attention to the biblical text.

2. You should be observing the biblical text in the original languages.

3. Patterns in the text are more important than word studies.

4. The New Testament’s use of the Old Testament is the key to understanding how prophecy works.

5. The Bible must be interpreted in context, and that context isn’t your own or that of your theological tradition; it is the context that produced it (ancient Near East/ancient Mediterranean).

6. The Bible is a divine human book; treat it as such.

7. If a passage is weird, it’s important.

8. Don’t hire someone to stock the grocery shelves who can’t read the labels. Or: don’t put your meds in the daily pill tray unless you can read the instructions.

9. If, after you’ve done the grunt work of context-driven exegesis, what the biblical text says disturbs you, let it.

10. Build a network of exegetical insights you can keep drawing upon; the connections are the result of a supernatural Mind guiding the human writers.
